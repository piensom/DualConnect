# Dual Connect - Usability Test Plan

## Test Overview

**Objective**: Evaluate the usability, clarity, and effectiveness of the Dual Connect MVP with representative users.

**Duration**: 30-45 minutes per session
**Number of Participants**: 5-8 users
**Test Format**: Task-based usability testing with think-aloud protocol
**Location**: Remote (video call) or in-person

---

## Test Goals

### Primary Goals
1. Assess whether users can successfully find relevant dual education programs
2. Evaluate clarity and comprehensibility of content (especially for parents)
3. Identify usability issues and friction points
4. Measure user satisfaction and likelihood to recommend

### Success Metrics
- **Task completion rate**: >80% of participants complete core tasks
- **Time on task**: Users find programs within 3 minutes
- **Error rate**: <3 errors per user
- **Satisfaction score**: Average >4/5 on post-test survey
- **Subjective ease**: >80% rate platform as "easy" or "very easy"

---

## Participant Recruitment

### Target Participants (5-8 total)

**Group 1: International Applicants (3-4 people)**
- Aged 16-25
- From non-EU countries
- German level A1-B2
- Interested in vocational training or dual study
- Mix of those in Germany and abroad

**Group 2: Parents of International Applicants (2-3 people)**
- Parents with children aged 14-25
- Limited knowledge of German education system
- Varying German language levels
- From diverse countries (Syria, India, Turkey, etc.)

**Group 3: Advisors/Stakeholders (1 person) - Optional**
- Career counselor or IHK representative
- Works with international applicants
- Can provide professional perspective

### Recruitment Methods
- Post in international student groups (Facebook, WhatsApp)
- Contact through integration support organizations (Diakonie, Caritas)
- Ask instructors for connections
- Offer small incentive (€10 gift card or certificate of participation)

### Screening Questions
1. Are you or your child interested in vocational training or dual study in Germany?
2. What is your German language level? (A1-C2)
3. Are you currently in Germany or abroad?
4. Have you used platforms like Ausbildung.de or Make it in Germany before?

## Test Script

### Introduction (5 minutes)

**Moderator says:**

"Hello! Thank you for participating in this usability test. My name is [Name], and I'll be guiding you through this session today.

We're testing a new website called Dual Connect that helps international families find dual education and vocational training programs in Germany. We want to understand how easy it is to use and whether it meets your needs.

**Important things to know:**
- We're testing the website, not you. There are no right or wrong answers.
- Please think out loud as you complete tasks. Tell me what you're looking at, what you're thinking, and any questions that come to mind.
- If something is confusing or doesn't work as expected, that's valuable feedback for us.
- This session will take about 30-40 minutes.
- We'll record your screen to review later, but your data will remain confidential.
- You can stop at any time if you feel uncomfortable.

Do you have any questions before we begin?

[Wait for consent]

Great! Let's start."

---

### Background Questions (5 minutes)

**Moderator asks:**

1. Can you tell me a bit about yourself and your interest in dual education in Germany?
2. Have you searched for training or study programs before? If so, what websites did you use?
3. What has been most challenging about finding information on dual education?
4. What device do you usually use to search for information? (computer, phone, tablet)

**Moderator notes:**
- User's background and context
- Prior experience with similar platforms
- Pain points and expectations

---

### Task Scenarios

### Task 1: Find a Program (10 minutes)

**Scenario for Applicant:**
"Imagine you're interested in working with computers and technology. You want to find a dual education program in IT, preferably in Berlin, and you have B1 German level. Use this website to find a suitable program."

**Scenario for Parent:**
"Your child is interested in becoming a nurse or working in healthcare. They have B2 German and want to study in Berlin or nearby. Help them find a healthcare program using this website."

**Success Criteria:**
- User navigates to search page
- Uses filters correctly (field, location, language)
- Finds and clicks on a relevant program

**Observation Points:**
- Does user find the search function easily?
- Do filters make sense?
- Is filtering intuitive?
- Does user understand program cards?

**Think-Aloud Prompts:**
- "What are you looking for now?"
- "What do you expect to happen when you click that?"
- "Is this what you expected to see?"

---

### Task 2: Understand Program Details (5 minutes)

**Scenario:**
"You've found a program that looks interesting (e.g., SAP Fachinformatiker). Imagine you want to apply. Find out what requirements you need to meet and how to contact the company."

**Success Criteria:**
- User clicks on program detail page
- Finds requirements section
- Locates contact person information

**Observation Points:**
- Is information hierarchy clear?
- Can user find requirements quickly?
- Is contact information prominent?

**Think-Aloud Prompts:**
- "Where would you look for requirements?"
- "What do you think this means?"
- "Do you have all the information you need to apply?"

---

### Task 3: Parent Information (For Parents Only) (5 minutes)

**Scenario for Parents:**
"You want to understand what dual education actually is and whether it's a good option for your child. Find information that explains this in simple terms."

**Success Criteria:**
- User navigates to "For Parents" page
- Reads and understands the concept
- Finds visual explanations or FAQ

**Observation Points:**
- Is "For Parents" section easy to find?
- Is language simple enough?
- Are visual aids helpful?

**Think-Aloud Prompts:**
- "Does this explanation make sense to you?"
- "Is there any jargon that's confusing?"
- "What else would you want to know?"

---

### Task 4: Find Funding Information (5 minutes)

**Scenario:**
"You're concerned about costs. Find out if there's financial support available for international students in dual education programs."

**Success Criteria:**
- User navigates to funding section or finds funding info on program page
- Understands funding options available
- Knows how to apply

**Observation Points:**
- Is funding information easy to locate?
- Is it clear and understandable?
- Does user know next steps?

**Think-Aloud Prompts:**
- "Where would you expect to find funding information?"
- "Do you understand what BAB or BAföG is?"
- "Does this give you enough information?"

---

### Task 5: Contact an Advisor (Optional) (3 minutes)

**Scenario:**
"You have more questions and want to talk to someone who can help. Find a way to contact an advisor who speaks your language."

**Success Criteria:**
- User finds contact page
- Identifies advisor with language skills listed
- Knows how to reach out

**Observation Points:**
- Is contact page accessible?
- Is language information prominent?
- Are contact methods clear?

---

### Post-Task Questions (5 minutes)

**Moderator asks:**

1. **Overall impression**: What are your first impressions of this website?
2. **Ease of use**: On a scale of 1-5 (1=very difficult, 5=very easy), how easy was it to use?
3. **Clarity**: Was the information clear and understandable?
4. **Comparison**: How does this compare to other platforms you've used (if any)?
5. **Missing information**: Was there anything you expected to find but didn't?
6. **Language**: Was the language level appropriate for you?
7. **Design**: What did you think of the visual design and layout?
8. **Likelihood to use**: If this were a real platform, would you use it? Why or why not?
9. **Improvements**: What's the one thing you would change or improve?

---

### Wrap-Up (2 minutes)

**Moderator says:**

"Thank you so much for your time and feedback! Your input is incredibly valuable and will help us improve the platform for international families like yours.

Before we finish:
- Do you have any final comments or questions?
- May we contact you again if we have follow-up questions?
- [Provide incentive/thank you gift if applicable]

Thank you again, and best of luck with your education journey!"

## Analysis Plan

### Quantitative Metrics
- Task success rate (%) for each task
- Average time per task
- Number of errors per user
- Average satisfaction rating
- Net Promoter Score (NPS)

### Qualitative Analysis
- Common pain points (grouped by theme)
- Positive feedback themes
- Feature requests
- Language/clarity issues
- Navigation confusion

### Severity Rating for Issues
- **Critical (P0)**: Prevents task completion, must fix
- **High (P1)**: Causes significant frustration, should fix
- **Medium (P2)**: Minor inconvenience, nice to fix
- **Low (P3)**: Cosmetic or preference, backlog

## Iteration Plan

### After Testing: What to Do with Feedback

**Step 1: Triage Issues (Week 1)**
- Categorize by severity
- Identify quick fixes vs. larger changes
- Prioritize based on impact and effort

**Step 2: Implement Changes (Week 2)**
- Fix critical issues immediately
- Plan high-priority improvements
- Document lower-priority items for backlog

**Step 3: Validate Changes (Optional)**
- Quick follow-up test with 1-2 users
- Verify fixes worked
- Check for new issues introduced

**Step 4: Document Learnings**
- Update design documentation
- Share insights with team
- Incorporate into final report

## Tips for Successful Testing

### For Moderators:
1. **Stay neutral**: Don't lead users or defend design choices
2. **Encourage thinking aloud**: "Keep talking, tell me what you're thinking"
3. **Probe for clarity**: "Can you say more about that?"
4. **Don't help too quickly**: Let users struggle a bit (that's where you learn)
5. **Thank participants often**: Show appreciation for their time

### Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- ✗ Explaining how things work before the user tries
- ✗ Asking leading questions ("That was easy, right?")
- ✗ Getting defensive about negative feedback
- ✗ Rushing participants through tasks
- ✗ Forgetting to record or take notes

### What Good Feedback Looks Like:
- ✓ "I didn't expect that button to be there"
- ✓ "I'm confused about what this means"
- ✓ "This is much clearer than [competitor]"
- ✓ "I'd want to see [feature] here"
